    How it works

    Every route has a rhythm.

    Airlines fill and empty in the same pattern most years. School holidays, winter, conference season: it repeats. We measure it and tell you the gaps.

    Years of real flight counts

    Government agencies publish how many seats flew and how many were filled, route by route, every month.

    The usual high and low months

    We work out how far each month usually sits above or below the rest of the year, route by route.

    Tested on past years

    Each route gets a trust score from how well its pattern has held up in past years, so you know which ones to lean on.

    One honest caveat: the public data counts the whole plane, not the business cabin. A quiet plane is still the best free signal for cheaper business fares, open reward seats and upgrades that clear.

      Questions

      Good to know.

      Does it show live seats on my flight?

      No. It shows how full a route usually is in each month, from past years. It doesn't check live availability or prices. Always check with the airline before you book or bid.

      Is it about business class only?

      The government data counts the whole plane. When the whole plane is quiet, business fares tend to drop, reward seats open up and upgrade bids win for less, so it is the best free guide we have.

      How often is it updated?

      About once a month. Each agency publishes two to three months behind, so the newest month shown is usually from a little while ago. The app picks up new data by itself.

      What does the trust score mean?

      It shows how well a route's pattern has held up in past years. 50 and up is reliable, 25 to 49 is some help, under 25 is weak.

      What does it cost?

      Nothing while we test it. There are no ads and no account.

      Is it connected with any airline?

      No. Airline names are only used to describe the data. Quiet Flights isn't connected with, or endorsed by, any airline or agency.
